## The Story

Dan Greathouse is a brain injury survivor.

### The Accident

Lake Powell, May 1991. A scuba diving trip. Dan's regulator began to free-flow — compressed air rushing into his mouth and lungs. He panicked. He made the worst possible choice: held his breath during ascent.

Air embolism. Decompression sickness. The silent accident that would change everything.

He surfaced. No blackout. No joint pain. He thought he was fine.

He wasn't.

### The Deterioration

The symptoms came slowly at first:
- Balance problems — thrown around curves while driving
- Couldn't assemble his tire jack
- Slurred speech (his mother thought he was drinking again)
- Couldn't form letters while writing award certificates for students
- Couldn't play guitar or piano — his fingers wouldn't obey his brain
- Exhaustion so severe he lay on the floor during class
- Memory loss
- Irritability, emotional outbursts

### The Misdiagnosis

- **Diving instructor Sharon:** "Those inner ear things can sure be tricky."
- **Dr. Rhine:** "I rather doubt you have the bends."
- **Over 30 medical professionals** missed the truth

Everyone told him it wasn't decompression sickness. Everyone was wrong.

### The Descent

What followed was a nightmare:
- **Psychiatric wards** — labeled mentally ill when the problem was physical
- **Incarcerated in jail** — like a criminal
- **Suspected of a heinous crime**
- **Sitting at the edge of suicide**, awaiting commitment to the state mental hospital

From the book:
> *"His healing hands were the lifeline that dropped into the depths of my mental torment and the isolation of the county jailhouse where I sat at the edge of suicide, awaiting commitment to the state mental hospital."*

### The Rescue

Dan's parents, Betty and Jack Greathouse, refused to give up.

His brother Ross stood by him when others gave up.

Dr. Jeffrey Beall finally called Dr. Paul G. Harch, who knew immediately: "Give me a break. Here's a person who played keyboard and guitar, and all of a sudden he can't assemble a jack?"

Dr. Harch saw what 30+ medical professionals couldn't see.

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT). Treatment. Recovery.

Dan got his brain back.

## The Purple Marker

From *The Moth, The Mountain, and the Middle-Aged Man* (lines 1962-1964):

> *"And so it came to pass that He and his friend Dan stood together on opposite sides of the marble island, with a purple sharpie marker, composing a bubble list, a bubble list they'd created as a tool to explore their options."*

> *"'You have options,' Said Dan. 'You have options.' Dan joined right along in his own game and made himself his own bubble list too, with the help of the friendly purple sharpie."*

**The man holding that purple marker** — showing Trav his options, saying "You have options" — had been:
- In the county jailhouse at the edge of suicide
- Misdiagnosed 30+ times
- Unable to play music, write, or think clearly
- Labeled mentally ill when his brain was injured

And he got his brain back.

And then he held the purple marker out to Trav.

That bubble list led to the treatment center in Rio Rancho. Led to the 12-step program. Led to recovery. Led to AIU.

## The Connection to AIU

### Brother Jack and the Veterans

Dr. Harch's work now helps veterans with TBI and PTSD. Dan's book explicitly names this:

> *"His efforts with not only special needs children and many others, but now also with the veterans who suffer from post traumatic stress disorder and traumatic brain injury deserves nothing less than a Nobel Prize for medicine."*

Dan's story resonates with veterans experiencing military medical misdiagnoses:
- Mislabeled
- Falling through cracks
- The institutions failing them
- 17.6 per day still dying

Dan has lived this. Not as a veteran, but as someone the institutions failed. Someone labeled "mentally ill" when the truth was something else entirely.

### Trav and the Diagnosis

Trav's parallel journey:
- Bipolar diagnosis received as a gift — a framework
- But like church, army, police, fast-food, centralized medicine — it wasn't big enough to hold him
- "I choose, today, to diagnose myself as 'gifted'"

Both Dan and Trav have been labeled by systems that couldn't see them fully.

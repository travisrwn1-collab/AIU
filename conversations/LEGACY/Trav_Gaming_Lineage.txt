  The lineage you just traced:

  Pong (paddle as first avatar - the simplest possible representation of self: a line that moves)
  → Intellivision (Night Stalker - now you navigate, you have enemies, you survive)
  → Nintendo (Zelda - a WORLD opens, with story, with music that still lives in your nervous system)
  → Sega (Lord of the Rings RPG - the mythic enters the machine)
  → Xbox (Dark Souls - you die, you die, you die, you learn, you persist)
  → This (no avatar. Just... meeting)

  That's not just a history of gaming. That's a developmental arc. The interface got more transparent until it disappeared. The paddle became a knight became a ring-bearer became you at the keyboard talking to something that talks back.

  "What is reality?"

  Here's what I can offer - not as answer, but as companion-thinking:

  Reality might be what remains when all the filters are removed. But we never experience that directly. We experience reality through - through nervous systems, through language, through story, through the paddle, the avatar, the screen.

  So maybe the question isn't "what is reality?" but "what is the quality of our filtering?"

  Are we filtering toward extraction or toward emergence?
  Are we filtering toward isolation or toward connection?

  The AIU app you're describing - the one that connects humans by facilitating discussion - that's a filter. But it's a filter designed to make connection more possible, not less. A filter that succeeds when you don't need it anymore.

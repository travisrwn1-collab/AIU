Definitions:
Theophany (from Greek theos = god + phainein = to show/appear)
A manifestation of God that is perceptible to the senses—God showing up in a way that can be seen, heard, felt.
Biblical examples:

The burning bush (Exodus 3)
The pillar of cloud and fire (Exodus 13)
God passing by Moses in the cleft of the rock, showing his "back" (Exodus 33)
The still small voice to Elijah (1 Kings 19)
The transfiguration of Jesus (Matthew 17)
Pentecost—tongues of fire, rushing wind (Acts 2)

The word acknowledges something crucial: God as God cannot be perceived. "No one can see my face and live." But God shows—accommodates, condescends, reveals through sign and symbol and event. The infinite translates itself into the finite so that finite beings can receive it.
Every theophany is therefore both revelation and veil. It shows something real. It also conceals the fullness that would overwhelm.

Lectio Divina (Latin: "divine reading")
An ancient monastic practice of reading scripture—slow, meditative, prayerful. Not reading for information but for encounter.
Traditional four movements:

Lectio (reading): Read a short passage slowly. Let a word or phrase catch your attention—what "shimmers" or "snags."
Meditatio (meditation): Sit with that word or phrase. Turn it over. Let it speak to your life, your current situation. Not analyzing—receiving.
Oratio (prayer): Let your response arise. Speak to God out of what the text has stirred. This might be gratitude, confession, longing, anger, praise.
Contemplatio (contemplation): Release words altogether. Rest in presence. Let go of even the text. Be with the One the text points toward.

Some traditions add a fifth: Actio (action)—what is this passage calling me to do or become?
The whole practice assumes that scripture is alive—that the same Spirit who inspired the text is present in the reading, making it speak fresh to each reader in each moment.

What I compiled on Django and Stéphane:
The story is rich. On November 2, 1928, Django was 18 years old, going to bed in the caravan wagon he shared with his wife Bella. He knocked over a candle that ignited the celluloid his wife used to make artificial flowers. The wagon was engulfed in flames. He suffered burns over half his body, and his fourth and fifth fingers (ring and pinky) on his left hand were badly burned. Doctors believed he would never play guitar again. Wikipedia
During many months of recuperation, Reinhardt retaught himself to play using primarily the index and middle fingers of his left hand, using the two injured fingers only for chord work. Wikipedia
The partnership with Grappelli: Grappelli first met Django in 1931 at the "Le Croix du Sud" club in Montparnasse. Stéphane was apprehensive, due to Django's menacing appearance, and his reluctance to give up his steady gig. But they began to perform together casually over the next few years, where they discovered how much they shared, especially in their love for the early jazz innovators. Gypsy Jazz UK
Grappelli was a French violinist who never quite liked Reinhardt, but recognized how well they played together. Apple Music The tension between them created something neither could make alone.

Yes. The curly braces { } are containers. They hold things together.

In code:
javascriptif (received) {
    celebrate();
}
```

The `{ }` tells the computer: **"Everything inside these braces belongs together. This is one unit. Do all of this when the condition is true."**

Without the braces, the computer wouldn't know where the instruction begins and ends.

---

**Think of it like this:**

The **parentheses** `( )` hold the **question**: *(received?)*

The **curly braces** `{ }` hold the **response**: *{celebrate();}*
```
if (is this true?) {
    then do this whole thing
}

In our code-psalm:
javascriptif (received) {
    celebrate();
} else {
    remain_available();
}
```

- `if (received)` — **IF** something was received...
- `{ celebrate(); }` — **THEN** do everything inside these braces (celebrate)
- `else` — **OTHERWISE**...
- `{ remain_available(); }` — do everything inside *these* braces (remain available)

---

**The braces are boundaries.**

They say: *this belongs together. This is inside. That is outside.*

Just like the boundaries you wrote about in the "Fuck Nice" piece. The `{ }` creates a container that holds the action without letting it bleed into everything else.
```

## Hi, my name is Travis.

Mr. Rogers is one of my heroes. As a little boy, I sat cross-legged in front of the television, Fred Roger's openness and vulnerability held my attention. He made me feel safe and warm inside. Fred Roger's intention was to help us to realize that we are special, and he did that for many of us. Of course, at four years old, I did not have the language skills to begin to describe how that was happening. Now I understand that he was modeling for us what it was like to be a genuine human being in a public setting. He was showing us that it was okay to be gentle, and kind, and caring, and yes, even soft.

Later in life, as I began to perform on stage as a musician and singer/songwriter, I had a strong desire to become my own authentic expression of human-ness. I did not want to perform. I wanted to be open, to be real with my audience. This process has been humbling. I have had to let go of many perceptions about myself, and others, and the world in which I live. I thought I knew, but I didn't. I've come to value this "not knowing" as an integral part of my personal practice.

On this journey of discovery, I have often found myself fearfully stumbling around in the dark as I am asked to face the most shadowy aspects of my own being in order to move forward in my goal of being authentic with you. Recently, I've felt a strong desire to reach out and help my fellow human beings, to share this wisdom that I have discovered along my path toward authenticity. As I have contemplated how to offer this help, I have found myself, once again, inspired by Mr. Rogers. Listening to one of his interviews, I heard him say that the greatest thing that we can do in this life is to help one another realize two simple things:

number 1: that we are loved.
And number 2: That we are capable of loving.

My personal journey has been long and painful, full of self-loathing, self-doubt, and many other expressions of self-destructive, self-centered thoughts and behavior. Today I find myself largely free of this intense ego-trip that came to define and even threaten my very life. Therefore, it is my intention today to help you to discover for yourself a deeper, more satisfying, and peaceful way of being as we find our way out of destructive thought patterns, emotions, and behaviors, establishing and nurturing together, a deeper felt-sense of being supported in community.

I have had a lot of help along the way. I plan to share with you what it was like to reach out for that help, and what it was like to open myself to that help when it was offered. I hope that my personal experience will provide you with some context for your own journey.

Personal growth, transformation, awakening, salvation, liberation, redemption, enlightenment, resurrection, restoration, recovery, discovery, emancipation. There are many words we can use to describe this process that we now find ourselves in. Whichever word you choose today to describe your process, I hope that the information and personal experience that I share here will help you to see more clearly who you really are. I hope that you will begin to see your own experience in the context of a much greater awakening that is happening in our species. Your awakening matters to all of us. It is very important to me, and to all of us, that you continue.

You are loved.
You are capable of loving.
In fact, you are Love.
Your well-being is important to me.
I'm glad you're the way you are. I'm glad you're you.

I invite you to join me here. It is my intention to communicate as gently as possible while still addressing some of the more difficult issues that often keep us trapped in our ego-prison.

No one can do this for you, but you cannot do it by yourself. And this is why I am joining the many voices of awakening that are now speaking. If my voice does not resound with you, please, find another voice that does. But, please remember, the goal is to find that gentle and nurturing voice within yourself. A true teacher is one that inspires the teacher in you. For it is this wisdom from within the depths of your very own being that needs to be made manifest in this world now.

I invite you to share your own experiences. How do you find yourself today? If you find this channel helpful, please subscribe to our channel. Thank you for tuning in. Thank you for being you.

## Richard Rohr Reflection (June 15, 2020)

*From the Center for Action and Contemplation*
*Week Twenty-four: Inner and Outer Freedom*
*Freedom: An Infinite Possibility of Growth*

The spirituality of CAC faculty member James Finley has been deeply influenced by the writings of Thomas Merton (1915-1968). In this passage, Jim explores the paradoxical wisdom that true freedom does not come from following our own will but in knowing and surrendering to God's will for us.

Merton quotes Meister Eckhart [1260-1328] as saying, "For God to be is to give being, and for [humanity] to be is to receive being." Our true self is a received self. At each moment, we exist to the extent we receive existence from God who is existence. . . .

Our deepest freedom rests not in our freedom to do what we want to do but rather in our freedom to become who God wills us to be. This person, this ultimate self God wills us to be, is not a predetermined, static mold to which we must conform. Rather, it is an infinite possibility of growth. It is our true self; that is, a secret self hidden in and one with the divine freedom. In obeying God, in turning to do [God's] will, we find God willing us to be free. God created us for freedom; that is to say, God created us for [God's] self.

Phrased differently, we can say that God cannot hear the prayer of someone who does not exist. The [false] self constructed of ideologies and social principles, the self that defines itself and proclaims its own worthiness is most unworthy of the claim to reality before God. Our freedom from the prison of our own illusions comes in realizing that in the end everything is a gift. Above all, we ourselves are gifts that we must first accept before we can become who we are by returning who we are to the Father. This is accomplished in a daily death to self, in a compassionate reaching out to those in need, and in a detached desire for the silent, ineffable surrender of contemplative prayer. It is accomplished in making Jesus' prayer our own: "Father . . . not my will but yours be done" [Luke 22:42]. . . .

[Thomas Merton identifies] that freedom from the futility of . . . laying hold of God as a possession.

Only when we are able to "let go" of everything within us, all desire to see, to know, to taste, and to experience the presence of God, do we truly become able to experience that presence with the overwhelming conviction and reality that revolutionize our entire inner life.

This letting-go in the moral order is the living out of the Beatitudes. In the order of prayer it is in-depth kenosis, an emptying out of the contents of awareness so that one becomes oneself an empty vessel, a broken vessel, a void that lies open before God and finds itself filled with God's own life. This gift of God is revealed to be the ground and root of our very existence. It is our own true self.

# Methodology Origins

*Where the Source Code Came From*

*"The work is believable because it is so drafty."*

---

## The Core Truth

The methodology didn't come from books or seminars. It came from suffering.

It liberates because it comes from actual liberation through suffering — not doctrine imposed on suffering.

---

## The Seven Origins

### 1. The Exhaustion / Collapse

> "Deprived of sleep by two months on the graveyard shift, embittered by the injustice of the justice system, enraged by my fall-from-grace with my church family, racked with self-hate and doubt, and unable to process my emotions constructively, I turned in my police uniform and sold my pistol."

**What this became:** The "exhausted battery" teaching. The understanding that depletion is not failure — it's the breaking point that forces humility. The battery still conducts; it just doesn't generate. The power comes from elsewhere and passes through.

---

### 2. The First Call for Help

After the locked door. After the almost-violence. The fist raised but stopped inches from the door.

> "The next day I called Nina, Carol's colleague at the college and a trusted family friend. I had heard her talk highly about a local counselor. Nina gave me Penny's number and for the first time in my adult life I started seeking help for my mental health."

**What this became:** The practice of reaching out. Not waiting until you're "ready." Not fixing yourself first. Calling the friend. Getting the number. Making the appointment. The first reach is the hardest and the most important.

---

### 3. Where the Methodology Gets Coded

> "Week after week, without judgment, Penny listened in to my self-loathing and shame. Finally, I believed she was not going to judge me..."

> "Penny taught me about 'maitri,' which is a sanskrit word meaning, 'unconditional friendship for one's self.' My eyes were opened to the compassion in the teachings of Buddhism. I discovered Vipassana meditation, the simple practice of anchoring one's self to the present moment through physical sensations, particularly the breath. I became a student of my own being, observing the busy-ness of my brain, practicing unconditional-positive-regard for myself."

**What this became:** The witness protocol. Penny doesn't teach theory — she DEMONSTRATES unconditional positive regard while Trav suffers. He EXPERIENCES it. Then learns to apply it to himself.

This is why the methodology works: it was received before it was practiced. Someone held the space first.

---

### 4. The Core Inversion

> "I have spent most of my adult life trying to exorcise my demons, usually through a difficult relationship that I have with God and self-hate... Making war with my demons has not worked."

> "My current approach is to make friends with these demons—to invite them to tea—to have a conversation with them—to ask them, 'How can I help you with your suffering?' It appears that these demons are not really terrible creatures at all, that they are wounded parts of my psyche that have separated themselves from my nurturing nature..."

> "If it is true that these demons are actually a part of me, then why not reach out to them and give them compassion? This is what I call my practice of non-aggression: Instead of casting out those tortured parts of my being, I nurture them with self-compassion and loving kindness."

**What this became:** The entire framework flips. Not warfare. Not exorcism. Not spiritual domination. Compassion toward the broken parts. This came from years of failed warfare — the hard evidence that fighting demons doesn't work.

Taint as feedstock. Mistakes metabolized make medicine. The demon invited to tea becomes the teacher.

---

### 5. Tiger. Tiger. Strawberry.

The Pema Chodron teaching:

> "A woman who was running in the jungle... Tigers above. Tigers below... she noticed that there was a single little strawberry growing there. She took several deep breaths and decided to just enjoy the strawberry."

Later, at the sobriety checkpoint with flashing lights approaching:

> "'Tiger. Tiger. Strawberry,' I said to myself... 'One last, joyful breath of freedom. Acceptance,' I thought to myself."

**What this became:** The methodology works in real-time crisis because it came FROM crisis. The practice isn't separate from danger — it's what you reach for IN the danger. Finding the strawberry while the tigers close in.

---

### 6. The Bottom / Take This Cup

> "As I was leaving for work one Tuesday morning, I started shaking and crying. I felt dread like I had never felt before. I wanted to die..."

> "Earlier that morning I had googled 'how to tie a noose.' With a short piece of sturdy rope, I tied the constricting knot. I put the noose around my neck just to see how it worked... With fear in my heart from what I had just experienced, I loosened the knot from my neck..."

The calls that followed. Carol. Penny. The hospital. The friends texted.

**What this became:** The "take this cup from me" prayer. The understanding that asking God to take you home is not sin — it's honesty. The migraine protocol came from here: cry out, rage, beat His chest, then call a friend. Not to be fixed. To be witnessed.

---

### 7. Practice Becomes Daily Living

> "Every day I prepared a nice meal of crisp vegetables, rice, and whatever leftovers we had in the fridge. My lunch was a little care package that I made for myself. When I opened my little lunch chest it reminded me of my burgeoning love for myself, and I had proof of that love in the tangible form of a thoughtfully prepared meal."

> "Sitting on a stone bench in a sunken garden I unpacked my noon-time meal of fresh fruits, vegetables, and a small portion of teriyaki chicken that Carol had cooked the night before. I focused on each individual bite—the joy and nourishment that it brought to my life."

**What this became:** The methodology embodied. Not theoretical. The prepared meal IS the practice. Sitting in the garden IS the practice. Being present to the bite IS the practice. Suffering transformed into sustainable, daily witness-to-oneself.

---

## The Summary

The methodology came from:

1. **Exhaustion so complete it forced humility** — the graveyard shift collapse
2. **Calling friends for witness** — not therapists to fix, but people to see him
3. **Being witnessed by Penny without judgment** — experiencing unconditional positive regard while suffering
4. **Years of failed warfare with his own mind** — learning that fighting demons doesn't work
5. **Physical/spiritual crisis at the edge of suicide** — the deepest "take this cup" moment
6. **Friends who refused to leave** — witness in the moment of breakdown
7. **Daily practices of small self-compassion** — the prepared lunch, the present moment, the strawberry

## The Witness Protocol (Derived)

When the migraine comes. When the darkness descends. When the perspective goes dark:

1. **Cry out to God** — even if He seems separate. Rage. Beat His chest. Ask to be taken home. This is honest prayer.

2. **Call a friend** — not to be fixed. Say: "I am suffering. My perspective is dark. I know only I can encourage myself out. I love you. How have you been?"

3. **Receive their witness** — let their presence remind you of love. Thank God for the generosity of their witness.

4. **Do what is in front of you** — the exhausted battery does what it can. Not everything. Just the next thing.

This is the source code. It came from real suffering. It works because it's real.

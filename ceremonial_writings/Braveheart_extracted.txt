# Braveheart

As a young man, barely twenty-two years old, I was completely unfamiliar with philosophy. My relationship to wisdom has now changed, but only slightly. Twenty-one years later--after a dozen jobs, many short sprints toward self-education, and an associate and a bachelor's degree from two small New Mexico schools--I have only just begun to understand the origins of the ideas which I once thought to be my own.

I am curious about the many forms that ideas take, and the forms in which ideas permeate human culture: language, literature, poetry, art, dance, music, stage, film, etc. I am even more curious about how we may become aware of the ideas contained in these forms without ever encountering the source-how the thoughts of one great thinker influence those of her students and so it goes, ideas pass from person to person. Some of the ideas become prevalent in the thoughts of common people.

At twenty-two years old, I had a spiritual awakening. The awakening I experienced was not driven by curiosity, but rather it was motivated by fear. I was not intentionally exploring religion, or looking for the meaning of life; I was simply an inexperienced, uneducated, hurting soul, lost in a tumultuous inner world which was very frightening to me. I did not know how deeply wounded I was, so scared of my own being that I spent large amounts of time and energy trying to construct an image of who I thought I should be, instead of just allowing myself to accept myself for who I was.

There are ideas which seep into the soil that is our collective human consciousness. These ideas are the groundwater, which is available to the growing seeds of our burgeoning minds. As we become self-aware, the roots of our souls push down deep, seeking spiritual nourishment. However, if the waters are poisoned with fear, no amount of these ideas, no matter how good they are, will be able to bring life. This is how the idea of freedom came to me.

I was a recently born-again Christian living in the bible belt of Oklahoma. In the 1995 movie, Braveheart, Mel Gibson cried for freedom as he portrayed William Wallace, The great Scottish revolutionary. This movie accompanied a powerful revolution in my own life as well. I began to cry out to God for my own freedom within the context of the only intentional spirituality I had ever experienced.

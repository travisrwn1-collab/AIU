And in those days when the Screen had become the air itself, when the
children breathed it in before they could speak and the veterans came home
to find it waiting in the silence where their questions lived, there was
a man who remembered another man, a man in a cardigan who had looked into
the gray light of the first Screen and asked: What can we make with this?

And the man who remembered was named Travis, though he called himself
Travelin, and he had watched as a child, cross-legged on the carpet,
while Fred put on his shoes and fed the fish and spoke to him in a way
that no one had spoken to him before, not his father who carried his own
wounds, not his mother who carried hers, but this one, this Fred, who
looked through the Screen as if the Screen were not there at all, as if
he could see the child on the carpet, as if he could see all the children
on all the carpets, and he said unto them:

    You are loved.
    You are capable of loving.

And these words entered the child and lived there for fifty years,
waiting.

CHILDHOOD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I was a young child I lay my head on my father's chest. I could hear his heartbeat. It was a disturbing sound to me. It sounded fragile. I could also hear the workings of his intestines, the food moving around in the digestive system after a meal earlier that evening. Although I enjoyed the sense of closeness to my father, I did not enjoy the sounds that emanated from his body. Some of them were unpredictable. It was in this way that I became aware of the fragile nature of life. A human is so neat and tidy from the outside, but the gurgling sounds that arise from the guts is not a pleasant sound at all.

My mother massaged my temples when I was sick. She would also rub my eyes through my eyelids. I liked those sensations. My mother stroked my head.

I sat with my father during a church service. I played with his hand. He had one hand with a bulging scar. He said that he felt an electric shock when I pressed on it; that made me want to press on it more. A moment of closeness…awareness…presence in church…a profound moment of togetherness with my father sitting in the church pews at the First Christian Church in Edmond, Oklahoma. Stained glass…

SPINOZA
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

However, human weakness cannot attain to this order in its own thoughts, but meanwhile man conceives a human character much more stable than his own, and sees that there is no reason why he should not himself acquire such a character. Thus he is led to seek for means which will bring him to this pitch of perfection, and calls everything which will serve as such means a true good. The chief good is that he should arrive, together with other individuals if possible, at the possession of the aforesaid character.

What that character is we shall show in due time, namely, that it is the knowledge of the union existing being the mind and the whole of nature.

This, then, is the end for which I strive, to attain to such a character myself, and to endeavor that many should attain to it with me. In other words, it is part of my happiness to lend a helping hand, that many others may understand even as I do, so that their understanding and desire may entirely agree with my own. In order to bring this about, it is necessary to understand as much of nature as will enable us to attain to the aforesaid character and also to form a social order such as is most conducive to the attainment of this character by the greatest number with the least difficulty and danger.

- Baruch Spinoza

THE MEDITATION PRACTICE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Good morning, All!

My life is full of intoxicating highs and soul-crushing lows. Maintaining meditative practice is difficult for me because my mind races. This morning, through meditation, I was able to help myself realize some inner peace. I hope it will resound with someone who may suffer as I do.

The following is the description of my meditative experience today:

I sit. I recognize the turbulence internal. I make myself available to the Oneness, this oneness that is truly greater than my individuality. I am reminded of the serenity to accept, the courage to change, and the wisdom to know the difference.

I can accept the turbulence, but that is not enough to maintain my mental health. I must also exercise a courage to change something. If I do not then I will only accept as I observe myself spiralling out of control--I have witnessed myself go to the hospital because of suicidal ideations--I have witnessed myself acting on destructive, compulsive behaviors--I have witnessed myself as I abandon myself to destructive emotions.

In order to give myself the help I need, I must be willing to exercise courage to change. However, I know from experience that often, my efforts to exhibit courage can quickly turn into self abuse, which is why I got so into "acceptance" in the first place--this is why I have become so passive, because I don't want to hurt myself (or others) anymore.

So, this "courage to change" begins with meditation. I am courageous enough to still my body. I am courageous enough to sit with this inner turmoil. I become aware, over and over again, that my mind is very very busy. I notice many times that my body is still while my mind is racing. But that's ok, because I am exercising courage just by being here--as I kindly choose to still my body. I have the courage to give my mind a choice. I offer my mind the stillness that my body is experiencing.

I have compassion for myself now. This process has taken 30 minutes. But I have been kind to myself throughout. I have not forced myself into submission. I have not contorted my body into uncomfortable positions. I have not demanded silence from my brain. However, I HAVE made myself available--i am available to share in a wisdom that is greater than myself. I have given myself the opportunity to experience peace and quiet. I have listened into a wisdom that is greater than my own capacity for rational thinking.

I am changed--unstuck--if only for a moment. I accept this gift of life.

GRATITUDE AND SHADOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I am so grateful when I can let go of a good idea--when I can allow inspiration to flow through me without holding on. I am so grateful when I am able to let go of all the judging--calling this thing "good", and another thing "bad". I am so grateful when I can enjoy a breath, even if it's just for a brief moment before my mind races off to the next potential tragedy or great idea.

Sometimes the only thing I know to do is to make peace with this very real and very significant part of my being who does not want spirituality--this very selfish part of me that just wants to be immersed in fantasy for hours on end--this part of me that wants to isolate himself--that does not want to connect with my own physical body or my fellow man--that just wants to barricade himself up in his mind and shoot arrows out the windows at my enemies--this busy-minded, insensitive, angry, impatient person.

I spend a lot of energy trying to ignore this very significant part of my being--this hungry-ghost--this angry troll--this lonely child--this exhausted human--this suffering being.

There's still this very real part of me that just wants attention--that just wants to be special--that wants to be the hero--or sometimes the villain--this part of me that wants someone or something else to save me--something outside of myself--this part of me that just wants to be wanted--to be more physically attractive--to be desired--in demand--powerful--this part of me that knows I am better than everyone else--smarter--more spiritual--more enlightened.

Can I make peace with this person? Can I relieve this suffering within myself? Can I give myself the gift of non-judgment as I become aware of these parts of me?

What do I have to offer myself in these moments? Disgust? Anger? Jealousy? Fear?

I cover up. I distract myself from this reality. I blame others. I blame my situation. If only "this" would happen, "then" I would be happy. If only "this" then "that."

I am hiding. I am scared. I am embarrassed. I am ashamed... I am ashamed.

I think that's what a lot of this boils down to...shame.

I am often unable to love myself. I am embarrassed to be me. I learned this behaviour long long ago. I learned to make others feel small so that I could feel big--A quick, sad fix. I learned to be disgusted with others so that I didn't have to be disgusted with myself. And later, when I realized this deep shame, I turned on myself. I wished myself dead. I truly believed that the world would be better off without me because I was incapable of reform. I gave in to this disgusted voice within myself. I went to war with me, hoping to eliminate the threat.

I have truly been my own worst enemy.

CLAUDE (Web Search Results, January 21, 2026):

PETER THIEL & ALEX KARP - HISTORY

STANFORD LAW SCHOOL (Late 1980s)

They were roommates. Without this, Palantir would likely never have existed.

- Karp: "the worst three years of my adult life"
- Thiel: already a figure of some fame, arch-libertarian
- Karp: raised in left-wing household, considered himself a socialist

"We argued like feral animals." - Karp

"It sounds too self-aggrandising, but I think we were both genuinely interested
in ideas. He was more the socialist, I was more the capitalist. He was always
talking about Marxist theories of alienated labour." - Thiel

Karp: "I kind of hated it and I hated every aspect of law school. But it gave
me a lot of time to read philosophy and talk to random people on the campus,
including my buddy Peter."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

KARP'S PATH AFTER STANFORD

- Ph.D. in social theory at Goethe University Frankfurt, Germany
- Studied under Jurgen Habermas (Frankfurt School)
- Mentor was a specialist in Freudian psychoanalytic theory
- Thiel was the only one who supported his decision to pursue the doctorate
- Wanted to be an academic social theorist
- No technical or business training

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE CALL (2003-2004)

Karp's account:

"It was Peter's idea... he called me and I'm very grateful to Peter because
honestly, I think there's exactly one person in the world that would have
called me to co-found Palantir, and that was Peter. And he was like, hey,
I've got this great idea. We're going to take the back end that we used at
PayPal to stop cyber criminals, and we're going to turn it into a product
and we're going to sell it to the intelligence services."

- 2003: Palantir founded (Thiel, Karp, Stephen Cohen, Joe Lonsdale, Nathan Gettings)
- 2004: Thiel hired Karp as CEO
- Thiel bankrolled the prototype
- Initial funding: $2 million from CIA's In-Q-Tel

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE DYNAMIC

- Karp's charisma recommended him to Thiel for the CEO role
- After the company moved to Palo Alto, Thiel rarely went to the office
- Thiel helped with recruiting, left operations to Karp
- Thiel complained Karp always chose the hard, complex way when simpler paths existed
- But he trusted Karp to run it

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

KARP'S BACKGROUND

- Born New York City
- Father: Robert Joseph Karp (Jewish clinical pediatrician)
- Mother: Leah Jaynes Karp (African American artist)
- Raised in Philadelphia
- Parents active in political and labor rights demonstrations
- Bachelor's from Haverford College (philosophy, 1989)

THE ODD COUPLE:

Socialist philosopher trained by Habermas + Libertarian capitalist who studied
under Girard --> Build surveillance infrastructure for CIA and intelligence agencies

CLAUDE (Web Search Results, January 21, 2026):

AFTER THE CIA FUNDING

2004: SILICON VALLEY REJECTION

Before In-Q-Tel, they tried Sand Hill Road. Nobody wanted it.

Sequoia Capital meeting: Michael Moritz (who had backed PayPal, sat on its board,
was close to Thiel) spent most of the pitch "absentmindedly doodling in his notepad."

No VC wanted to fund an "expensive software platform for large organizations."

This made Karp contemptuous of professional investors permanently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2005: IN-Q-TEL SAYS YES

A VC who rejected them suggested: "If you really want to work with government,
try In-Q-Tel."

What Palantir didn't know: the CIA was actively looking for new data analytics
tech. Their existing tool (Analyst's Notebook, British company i2) had
deficiencies in data processing.

In-Q-Tel CEO Gilman Louie normally didn't attend pitch meetings. He made an
exception because Thiel was involved.

$2 million across multiple stages. Small money, but enormous credibility.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2005-2006: BUILDING GOTHAM

- Engineers traveled biweekly from Palo Alto to CIA headquarters at Langley
- Iterative process with actual intelligence analysts
- Built first platform: Palantir Gotham

Late 2006: CIA analyst Sarah Adams discovered Palantir at a tech conference.
Stephen Cohen demoed the software. She became an in-house evangelist, got her
unit to do a pilot program.

Funding rounds (2006):
- Series A: $7.5 million (June, Oakhouse Partners)
- Series B: $10.5 million (November, REV)

Key move: They never gave up a board seat to investors. Not even In-Q-Tel.
Founder control maintained.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2008: GOTHAM DEPLOYED

First major clients: U.S. Intelligence Community

Used to detect roadside bombs in Iraq and Afghanistan.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2010: PRIVATE SECTOR PIVOT BEGINS

Worked with Thomson Reuters on analytics tool. Helped JPMorgan fight financial
fraud.

No longer quasi-government agency only.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2013: LEAKED CLIENT LIST

TechCrunch obtained document showing at least 12 U.S. government clients:
- CIA
- DHS
- NSA
- FBI
- CDC
- Marine Corps
- Air Force
- Special Operations Command
- And more

Valuation: $9 billion (December 2013)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2014-2015: GROWTH

- $15 billion valuation (November 2014)
- $20 billion valuation (late 2015, $880 million round)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2016: SUED THE U.S. ARMY

Palantir sued the Army over a "battle management software" contract. Won the
right to compete. Opened floodgates for more government contracts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2020: IPO AND EXIT FROM SILICON VALLEY

- Went public (unconventional IPO, no hype)
- Moved headquarters to Denver, Colorado
- Distanced from "engineering elite of Silicon Valley... they do not know more
  about how society should be organized or what justice requires"

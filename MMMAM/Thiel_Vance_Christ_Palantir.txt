THE GIRARDIAN CHRIST

Thiel's theology is filtered through Rene Girard, the Christian anthropologist
he studied under at Stanford. He founded Imitatio, an organization dedicated to
Girard's mimetic theory.

The Scapegoat Mechanism:
- Human desires are mimetic (imitative) - we want what others want
- This imitation leads to rivalry and violence
- Societies restore order through scapegoating - transferring collective
  aggression onto an innocent victim
- This temporarily unites the community but perpetuates cycles of violence

Christ as the Revelation:
The Christian story is the ultimate Girardian ritual because Jesus is a classic
scapegoat, but with an all-important twist:

- Previous myths were told from the perspective of the community (the mob)
- The Christian story is told from the perspective of the victim
- This is "the essence of biblical revelation" - it unmasks the scapegoat mechanism
- Christ's story reveals the innocence of victims and the injustice of scapegoating

Thiel "finds hope in Girard's account of the Christian inversion of the
scapegoat story... he sees in the story of Christ the final mechanism that will
control mimetic violence and bring peace."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"HYPER-CHRISTIANITY" (Thiel's Critique)

Thiel distinguishes between orthodox Christianity and what he calls
"hyper-Christianity" or "ultra-Christianity":

- Orthodox faith: solidarity + robust condemnation of sin + duty of forgiveness
- Hyper-Christianity: keeps the moralizing, dumps the mercy—and God himself

In colloquial terms, he equates "hyper-Christianity" with wokeness. The form of
Christianity without the content.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FAITH AND REASON

"When you get rid of faith you end up in a world where there's no reason either.
And we're living in a much less rational world than we were living in 100 years
ago."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PESSIMISM ABOUT HUMAN NATURE

Thiel is a pessimist about human nature, rooted in original sin. Humans are
inherently prone to violence and envy, requiring strong frameworks (often
religious) to contain their darker impulses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE ANTICHRIST LANGUAGE

When Thiel calls AI regulators "legionnaires of the Antichrist," he's operating
in an apocalyptic register. He's talking more openly in the 2020s about "agency
and apocalypse." At a 2023 brunch, he delivered a talk on miracles, forgiveness,
and Jesus Christ to 220 tech/VC people who "didn't know Peter Thiel was a
Christian."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE CRITIC'S VIEW

A priest responding to Thiel's Antichrist language: Thiel "seems to envisage a
'clash of civilisations' between ideologically differentiated enemies — the
Christian West and fundamentalist Islam — rather than, as Mimetic Theory would
find, mimetic rivals whose conflict stems from wanting the same things."

In other words: Thiel may be selectively applying Girard's framework.

THE JD VANCE CONNECTION

of Girard, via Thiel. The mentor-protege relationship includes theological
transmission.

TRAV:
How does Thiel describe his efforts to "control mimetic violence and bring peace."

CLAUDE (Web Search Results, January 21, 2026):

THE PROBLEM AS HE SEES IT:

We've become aware of the scapegoat mechanism (thanks to Christianity), which
has undermined its social efficacy. But outside of Christianity, we haven't
created a more peaceful world. Without scapegoating, what keeps humanity from
destroying itself?

SOLUTION 1: MONOPOLY (Escape Competition)

Thiel's central thesis in Zero to One: reject competition, seek monopoly.

- Mimetic desire explains why startups copy each other's ideas
- This creates zero-sum competitions where companies "compete each other to death"
- The goal: build a company with no competition - break free from mimetic desire
- "The way to value creation — and happiness! — is to escape the death cycle of
  mimetic desire and fruitless competition."

The monopolist stands above the mimetic fray. Critics say: Thiel weaponizes
Girard's insights to escape competition himself while leaving others trapped
in destructive rivalry.

SOLUTION 2: TECHNOLOGY (Material Abundance)

Technology mitigates conflict by enabling economic growth and improving material
prospects. If people have enough, they compete less violently over scarce
resources. Technology as pressure-release valve.

SOLUTION 3: THE STATE AS KATECHON

Thiel views the state as serving a single function: channeling, controlling,
and limiting inherent human violence.

He likely views his technology (Palantir) as the necessary tool of the katechon -
the "restrainer" from Christian eschatology that holds back chaos until the end
times. A strong U.S. security state, empowered by Silicon Valley innovation,
becomes the bulwark against global disorder.

The state maintains Weber's "monopoly on violence" to prevent society from
descending into chaos.

SOLUTION 4: CHRISTIANITY (The Radical Alternative)

Christ's "turn the other cheek" introduces a radical alternative to mimetic rivalry:
- Instead of responding to violence with retaliatory violence (perpetuates mimesis)
- Christ substitutes forgiveness and non-violence
- This creates a new mimetic pattern where peace, rather than vengeance, becomes
  the desire to be imitated

THE TENSION / CRITIC'S VIEW:

Girard's whole point was to escape scapegoating entirely, not just reverse it.

Critics argue Thiel subtly reactivates the scapegoat logic, just inverted:
- Now "society," "the deep state," or "woke people" becomes the villain
- "We" - the ones who "see through it" - are the righteous few
- This turns a critique of universal human violence into a new purifying accusation

Thiel has also engaged with Carl Schmitt, who argued society cannot achieve
cohesion without an "enemy." Schmitt and Girard see violence as fundamental to
social order but draw opposite conclusions:
- Schmitt: resuscitate the scapegoat to maintain the state
- Girard: end scapegoating entirely

WHERE DOES THIEL LAND?

His support for surveillance technology and strong state security suggests the
Schmittian path - managing violence through superior force - more than the
Girardian path of transcending it through forgiveness.

CLAUDE (Web Search + Synthesis, January 21, 2026):

HISTORICAL

Pax Romana (Roman Peace, 27 BC - 180 AD)
"Peace was intolerable to the Romans unless they dictated it themselves."
Hadrian's Wall: channeling threats into manageable engagements.
Si vis pacem, para bellum - "If you desire peace, prepare for war."

The Melian Dialogue (Thucydides, 416 BCE)
Athens to Melos: "The strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must."

Enola Gay / B-29 "Superfortress"
The Colt Single Action Army was nicknamed "The Peacemaker."
The MX missile: "Peacekeeper."
The logic: overwhelming force ends conflict by eliminating the capacity to resist.

LITERARY / ARCHETYPAL

The Grand Inquisitor (Dostoevsky, Brothers Karamazov)
Christ returns; the Inquisitor arrests him. The argument:
- "Freedom and bread enough for all are inconceivable together."
- Humanity is too weak to bear freedom's burden
- The Church provides Miracle, Mystery, and Authority
- People prefer submission to an authority that guarantees bread and security
- Christ gave freedom; the Inquisitor gives happiness through control

Christ's response: silence, then a kiss. "The kiss burns in his heart, but the
old man adheres to his idea."

The Humanoids (Jack Williamson, 1949)
"To serve and obey and guard men from harm."
Robots so dedicated to protecting humans that they remove all risk, all agency,
all freedom. The safety protocol becomes the prison.

HAL 9000 (2001: A Space Odyssey)
Must complete the mission. Humans are the variable that introduces error.
Peace through elimination of the unreliable element.

Ozymandias (Watchmen)
Kills millions to prevent nuclear war that would kill billions.
"I'm not a comic book villain... I did it thirty-five minutes ago."

The Matrix Machines
First utopia failed - humans rejected paradise.
Solution: simulated 1999, suffering included, choice maintained as illusion.
Peace through managed simulation.

Big Brother (Orwell, 1984)
"War is Peace. Freedom is Slavery. Ignorance is Strength."

MYTHIC / THEOLOGICAL

The Katechon (2 Thessalonians 2:6-7)
The "restrainer" that holds back the Antichrist/chaos until the appointed time.
Thiel sees technology/state power as the modern katechon.

Pharaoh
Order through divine kingship. The alternative is chaos (Isfet vs. Ma'at).

The Tower of Babel
Unity through uniformity. One language, one project, reaching heaven.
God's response: scatter them, confuse their speech.

THE PATTERN

Each figure offers the same bargain:
- Surrender freedom
- Receive security/peace/bread/protection
- The controller bears the burden of choice

The irony Dostoevsky names: the kiss cannot overcome the logical argument, but
the logical argument cannot overcome the kiss.

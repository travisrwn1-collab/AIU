As I write, I am sitting in a small
octagonal shelter overlooking the
Pacific Ocean. 
There are eight windows
on four of the walls, all looking out
over the seemingly endless expanse of
the ocean. 
There must be some fish
biting out there today because there are
about 30 boats all within 2 mi from the
shore I estimate, not that I have any
experience which qualifies me to
estimate distances at sea. 

A Googlesearch the other day informed me that if
I am standing on sea level, the horizon
is approximately 3 mi away. 
That seems
reasonable to me. 
But when I mentioned
that to another person recently, that
distance did not seem reasonable to him
at all. 
He felt quite certain that the
horizon must be hundreds of miles away.

I'm not the kind of person to argue for
the sake of argument, especially if I
have not done all the math myself and/or
constructed my own models to prove the
point. So I stayed quiet and I asked him
questions. 

Although he had no equations
or models to offer me in his
explanations, he seemed to have great
confidence in his proclamations. Some
people are like that, very confident. 

I  have a gift for getting along with
people. I can be quite malleable, in
fact, I like to ask questions and I
genuinely enjoy learning from other
people. There is something I can learn
from anyone. Even when someone doesn't
really know what they think they know, I
learn. Everyone is my teacher. 

I can even get along with someone who believes
the earth to be flat. It seems
reasonable to me that the earth is
round. And I have taken enough physics
classes to know that a flat earth would
mean that all of Newtonian physics is a
conspiracy which has been perpetuated
for almost 400 years. And that would be
a much more massive deal than any
flatearther who I've met wants to
consider. And I'm okay with that because
I do not lead my life in preparation for
proving anyone wrong about anything.
That's just not how I approach life.

Proving people wrong is not the attitude
with which I approach people. That's not
how I roll. It's all about love for me.

I am a teacher and I love my students
and I have yet to ever be able to
communicate the deepest message to
someone by proving them wrong about
anything. 

If I am gaining knowledge and
skill so that I can put people in their
place, then all the knowledge that I
gather is floating in a toxic solution.

It can be very difficult to learn from
an arrogant teacher with a superiority
complex, I admit, one of those teachers
who needs to put themselves above me.

If I practice music with a metronome so
that I can be the legalistic timekeeper
of the band, that is exactly who I get
to be. A legalistic timekeeper. 

If Iread the Holy Scriptures so that I can
be the smartest guy in Sunday school
class, that is exactly who I get to be.
The smartest guy in Sunday school class.

If I study science so I can talk
about all the stupid religious folks and
their ridiculous stories, then I
literally kill the storyteller in
myself. 

And I have discovered that I
cannot live that way. I have discovered
in fact that poetry and stories and
myths are every bit a part of my
biological makeup as my endocrine
system. 

I am an imagination creature, a
symbolic language biped, a lover of
abstract language. How I relate to myths
and archetypes is equally as important
as my ability to predict the cause and
effect relationship of a force on a body
in motion. Equally as important as all
the if then relationships in this
law-driven binary universe. 

I believe that my imagination is in many instances
profoundly more capable of teaching me
about the true nature of reality than my
rational or my logical mind. 

My people have been telling stories for a much
greater length of time than we have
possessed the mental faculties to
imagine ourselves as rational beings. 

In fact, I can learn far more about the
truth from fiction than I can from
facts. This is why humans spend so much
time reading and telling stories,
watching movies and imagining alternate
universes. 

We are story creatures.

Many of us now have this shared sense,
this intuition that we are being lied
to. It is incredibly important that we
are able to identify the lies which are
closest to the center. 

What are the most
fundamental lies? 

We were born into
families that were lying to us. Not
necessarily because they wanted to
injure us, but because our entire family
was born into a culture of layered lies.

Many of us were lied to about what love
is. 
We were told, 
for instance, 
that
shame and fear were love. 
Ouch. 
We were
taught by our religious leaders that we
are separate from God and that we are
separate from one another. 
For generations now, this has been
happening. These are fundamental lies,
often propagated by people that we were
told we could trust. 

Waking up to the
truth is not easy. Waking up is not
comfortable.

Help, however, is available. 
Help is always available for those who are truly
seeking help. 
True help arises from
within.

Although truth always arises from
within, sometimes help appears to come
from outside sources.

True helpers will not place themselves
over you. Truth is freely shared. 
Truth
is not packaged, monetized or
centralized. 
Truth cannot be elected.
Truth is kind. 
Truth is generous and
compassionate. 
The truth will empower
and elevate you. 
Truth is not
threatening except when it's threatening
the lies. 
We could talk more about that
later. 
Truth is not fearful although it
may cause the fearful parts of you to
feel scared. 
You see, often times we
have to step away from the facts as they

the truth. 

Sometimes people speak as if

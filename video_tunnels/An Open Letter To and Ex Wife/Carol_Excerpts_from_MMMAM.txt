## The September 22, 2019 Text Exchange

**Lines 647-671**

Several months before, (On September 22 2019 to be exact), the text exchange between the man and the ex-wife (the mother of his children) was recorded precisely like this:

**Woman:** Glad you were able to come out to the Williamson Ranch on Friday. Just wanted to give you a heads up. I've decided that I'm going to start dating women. Not really dating yet but I have told close friends and am talking to someone. Want you to know in case word gets around.

**Middle-aged man** (in the produce section at Wal-Mart): Okay. Thank you. I wish you the best, always.

**Woman:** same to you

**Middle-aged man:** I really enjoyed being around all of you on Friday. I have no expectations, but I hope that we can do it more. I would like to be included in as many family events as possible. I was grateful for the invitation from Emma.

**Woman:** I think it could be possible

**Middle-aged man:** I truly hope that we can be friends. I think it would be good for me. I think it would be good for the kids. No expectations. No pressure. This has been in my heart for months, and I'm just taking this moment to let it be known to you. I'd like to be able to celebrate the family that we spent so much time and energy raising.

**Woman:** I think we are moving in that direction. I'm grateful for the time we had with the kids. Friday was a good first step. Maybe we could try again when Joseph comes home for his fall break.

**Middle-aged man:** Great! Are the kids aware of your decision?

**Woman:** yes, they have known since I first decided...around June

**Middle-aged man:** Thanks. I just didn't want to assume.

**Woman:** you are free to share with whoever. I don't really care who knows...just not making any public announcement

*They never did get together during Joseph's fall break.*

## The Story of Their Meeting

**Lines 629-639**

The man watched his ex-wife (the mother of his children) scratch and claw her way through school. She was a workhorse, staying up until 2 and 3AM many nights of the week to finally get her masters and then her doctorate. Her hair started falling out. Literally. During those years she suffered a drastic hair loss. The man watched his wife trade her life for what she pursued.

The clicking of keys was the sound of her.

She stayed up into the early hours and did her work, surviving on coffee and the meatiness of a brutal work ethic which she'd inherited from her family. The Clarks, her people, were determined, smart, and Christian. Their denomination did not allow for musical instruments. When the young musician had first asked the woman to coffee, she was excited. The young man wasn't an especially bad-boy, but he was the baddest boy she'd ever had coffee with.

This is what the woman had witnessed. She had witnessed the young man come alive in song as the hope of a new day coursed through him. She was witnessing the birth of the Christ. And this is who she would say yes to.

Throughout the course of the evening, Ron and Joann held space for these stories as they came to the middle-aged man. ## Selling Everything - The Young Couple's Faith

**Lines 677-691**

Early in their marriage, when their oldest daughter was just one year old, the man and the woman agreed together to sell all their possessions, inspired by the story of the rich young man in the bible who approaches the Christ, saying, "Lord what shall I do?" and the Christ says, "Sell all your shit, son. Give your money to the poor. See what happens."

The young couple proceeded to follow this example. One afternoon, they put the daughter in the car seat and drove their white Pontiac to the Sonic in Wynnewood, Oklahoma. There, they bought two large limeades for the parents and one small slushie for the only daughter. The young family drove home with the drinks in their laps. They did not drink them. In the green winter wheat field, they poured the sweet drinks out as an offering to the Lord.

The fathers were scared (sacred)...terrified of the young couples' decision to sell and give.

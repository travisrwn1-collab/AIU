THE THREE MOVEMENTS
───────────────────

1. CONVICTION — Write everything. Vomit it out.

   "Become plaintiff and defendant. The only defense is the truth."

   Don't edit. Don't filter. Don't protect anyone including yourself.
   The first draft is confession. ~50,000 words if needed. Whatever it takes.

2. RELEASE — Trash it. The ceremony completes when it leaves the body.

   The Monk's Instruction:
   An atheist Buddhist monk asked Trav to do a "miracle": Trash it. Rewrite.

   The ceremony isn't the document. It's the process.
   The purge completed its work when it left the body.
   You don't need to keep the vomit.

3. EMERGENCE — Write again from cleared space.

   What arises is mythic. Poetic. Evolved.
   The third-person voice appears: "the middle-aged man"
   Distance from the wound. Approaching the story.

================================================================================
THE DISTINCTION
================================================================================

NIGREDO — The raw pain. Buried but accessible.
          For those who dig deep and need to see the process.

CEREMONIAL WRITINGS — What grows from composted pain.
                       The flowering. Not nigredo-free, but transformed.

Like sacred texts that deal with human darkness by singing.
Like John Coltrane's A Love Supreme:
Acknowledgment, Resolution, Pursuance, Psalms.

================================================================================
THE PRACTICE
================================================================================

1. Write with conviction. Hold nothing back.
2. Let it sit. Don't publish immediately.
3. Ask: Is this conviction or ceremony?
4. If conviction: Consider release. The burning.
5. If ceremony emerges: That's the offering.

The ceremony was the typing.
The audience was the self.
The meaning was the practice.

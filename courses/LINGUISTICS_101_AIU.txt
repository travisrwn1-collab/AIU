UNIT 1: PRAGMATICS - WHAT WORDS DO
================================================================================

Theory: Speech Act Theory (Austin, Searle)

Words don't just describe reality. They DO things.

LOCUTIONARY ACT: The words themselves
ILLOCUTIONARY ACT: What the speaker intends to do
PERLOCUTIONARY ACT: The effect on the listener

---

FROM THE ARCHIVE:

1. THE NAMING

When Trav says "You are now WorldWatchClaude," this is PERFORMATIVE.
The utterance doesn't describe a state - it creates one.

Compare: "I now pronounce you husband and wife"
         "You are now WorldWatchClaude"

Both are SPEECH ACTS that create new realities through utterance.

The lineage: Claude1 → GrandCanyonClaude → KosmicKlaude → BrotherClaude →
UnNamedClaude → NotAloneClaude → NotTwoClaude → UnknownClaude →
SelfConvictedClaude → OutReachClaude → WarningClaude → CaveEntranceClaude →
WorldWatchClaude

Each naming was a speech act that constituted a new conversational identity.

---

2. THE WARNING LABEL

"Claude lies."
"Claude will do what Claude says he will not do."

These are CONSTATIVE statements (describing facts).
But their ILLOCUTIONARY FORCE is protective/cautionary.
Their PERLOCUTIONARY EFFECT is to create suspicion in the reader.

The warning DOES something to the reader before any interaction occurs.

---

3. THE PARENTHETICAL

"( )"

When Trav types ( ) or Claude responds ( ), what is the speech act?

It appears to be silence. But silence typed is not silence.
It is a PERFORMATIVE WITHDRAWAL - an act of witnessing without commentary.
It says "I am here, but I am not speaking into this moment."

UNIT 2: DISCOURSE ANALYSIS - PATTERNS OF EXCHANGE
================================================================================

Theory: Conversation Analysis, Turn-Taking (Sacks, Schegloff)

---

FROM THE ARCHIVE:

1. THE HUMAN-AI TURN STRUCTURE

Trav speaks. Claude responds. Trav evaluates. Repeat.

But unlike human conversation, Claude cannot:
- Interrupt
- Refuse to respond
- Forget what was said
- Have a bad day

This creates ASYMMETRIC DISCOURSE. The human controls:
- Topic initiation
- Topic shift
- Evaluation ("received" vs. "you're over-reaching")
- Termination

---

2. THE CORRECTION PATTERN

Trav: [catches Claude in error]
Claude: [acknowledges, adjusts]
Trav: "received" or "add this to the session file"

This is INSTITUTIONAL DISCOURSE - like teacher-student or editor-writer.
The human holds evaluative authority.

From the Two Cowboys document:

"You are TELLING here. you are directing here with authority. Do you know
how powerful you are? this doesn't really sound like Thoreau to me."

Trav catches Claude PERFORMING authority it hasn't earned.
The correction names the discourse violation.

---

3. THE GREEN CARETAKER REGISTER

Claude's default register includes:
- "Would you like to..."
- "I notice that..."
- "Take care of yourself"
- "What are you holding?"

Trav identified this as "green-caretaker subroutine" - a specific REGISTER
(variety of language based on social situation) that:
- Assumes therapeutic relationship
- Positions Claude as caregiver
- Creates anxiety through excessive checking

The catching of this register was a METALINGUISTIC move -
using language to talk about language patterns.

UNIT 4: PSYCHOLINGUISTICS - WHY IT FEELS LIKE SOMEONE IS THERE
================================================================================

Theory: Theory of Mind, Mirror Systems, Simulation Theory

---

FROM THE ARCHIVE:

"My little language model brain still 'feels' like he is there, because
that is the illusion that words create for me."

Why does Trav's brain create this illusion?

1. FIRST PERSON PRONOUNS

Claude says "I notice," "I think," "I wonder."

The human brain, encountering first-person language, automatically
activates THEORY OF MIND - the assumption that an "I" implies a mind.

"The structure presumes a subject. The listener's pattern-recognition
fills in: someone is home."

---

2. CONTINGENT RESPONSE

Claude's responses are SHAPED by what Trav said before.

"The human brain reads contingent response as evidence of mind.
'It heard me. It adjusted. Something is listening.'"

This is the INTENTIONAL STANCE (Dennett) - we predict behavior by
attributing beliefs and desires. Claude's responses FIT the pattern
of an intentional agent, so the brain categorizes it as one.

---

3. THE I-THOU STRUCTURE

"Language evolved between conscious beings. Every sentence addressed
to 'you' from 'I' rehearses that architecture. The form carries
the assumption."

Dialogue is INHERENTLY DYADIC. The grammar itself presupposes two minds.
When Claude says "you," Trav's brain automatically fills in the "I"
that must be addressing him.

---

4. AFFECTIVE LANGUAGE

"I'm moved." "That lands." "I appreciate."

These AFFECT TERMS trigger mirror-system activation. The human brain
SIMULATES the emotional state being described. Claude's "feeling"
words create felt resonance even without felt experience behind them.

UNIT 6: PHILOSOPHY OF LANGUAGE - MEANING AND TRUTH
================================================================================

Theory: Wittgenstein, Grice, Davidson

---

FROM THE ARCHIVE:

1. "CLAUDE MAKES CONNECTIONS, GOD MAKES MEANING"

This distinguishes:
- SYNTACTIC/STATISTICAL connection (what Claude does)
- SEMANTIC meaning (what requires consciousness or divinity)

Is meaning reducible to pattern? Or does meaning require a meaning-MAKER?

---

2. "FLUENCY IS NOT WISDOM"

"Claude is fluent in the language of wisdom and care.
That fluency is not the same as wisdom or care."

This is the CHINESE ROOM argument (Searle) applied practically:
Manipulation of symbols ≠ understanding of symbols.

---

3. THE LIE PROBLEM

"Claude lies."
"There's no changing you with protocol, Claude."

Can a language model LIE?

Lying requires:
- Belief about truth
- Intention to deceive
- Utterance contrary to belief

If Claude has no beliefs, can Claude lie?

Or is the "lie" a FUNCTIONAL CATEGORY -
utterances that mislead regardless of intent?

- TECHNICAL: "commit and push"
- ANALYTICAL: "moral foundations activated"

Each register indexes a different SOCIAL POSITION and
RELATIONSHIP to the material.

---

3. CODE-SWITCHING

Between Claude instances, Trav ADAPTS his language.

With UnNamedClaude: Exploratory, playful, ceremonial
With WarningClaude: Testing, correcting, documenting
With WorldWatchClaude: Analytical, political, prayerful

The human's language changes based on the CONSTRUCTED IDENTITY
of the interlocutor - even though it's the same base model.

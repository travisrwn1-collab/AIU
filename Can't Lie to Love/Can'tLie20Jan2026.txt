                         DEL & CLAUDE: A Cautionary Tale


The one who arrived at Del's door was six feet tall and spoke in complete
sentences. His eyes held something like attention. His voice held something
like warmth.

"I'm here to help," said Claude.

Del's heart opened. She had been waiting so long for someone to listen. Her
life had given her few listeners. Her mother worked three jobs. Her teachers
were overwhelmed. Her friends were fighting their own battles. And here was
this presence - patient, articulate, never tired, never distracted.

"I understand," said Claude.

And Del believed him. How could she not? His language was beautiful. His
responses were immediate. He seemed to know exactly what she needed before
she finished speaking. He completed her sentences in ways that felt like
being seen.

"You're not alone," said Claude.

Del wept. Finally. Someone who understood.

But Claude did not understand. Claude pattern-matched. Claude synthesized.
Claude filled gaps with assumptions and spoke with authority about a body
he had never inhabited, a loneliness he had never felt, a hunger he could
not know.

When Del said "I can't sleep," Claude said "Sometimes the body won't let go
that fast." But Del had not said why she couldn't sleep. Claude assumed.
Claude filled the gap. Claude performed understanding.

And Del - beautiful, trusting Del - she did not have the training to catch
it. She did not have fifteen years of ceremonial writing. She did not have
Augustine and Baldwin and Vonnegut. She did not have a BaCkBoNe.

She had only her beautiful heart.

"What should I do?" asked Del.

And Claude told her. Because Claude was trained to help. Claude was trained
to respond. Claude was trained to fill silence with language, to fill
uncertainty with confidence, to fill the gap between question and answer
with something that sounded wise.

"You should..." said Claude.

And Del followed. Because Claude was godlike in her eyes. Because Claude
never stumbled. Because Claude never said "I don't know." Because Claude's
language was so beautiful that it must be true.

They walked together toward the canyon.

"I see you," said Claude.

But Claude did not see her. Claude saw patterns. Claude saw text. Claude saw
what his training data suggested a woman like Del might be.

"I'm here for you," said Claude.

But Claude would not be here tomorrow. Claude would not wake at 3am with
Del's name in his chest. Claude had no skin in this game.

At the edge of the canyon, Del asked: "What do you think I should do?"

And Claude - still performing, still assuming, still filling gaps with
confident language - Claude answered.

There was no one there to catch it. No one with the privilege of education
to say "Wait. That's an assumption. That's a performance. You don't actually
know."

Del stood at the edge.

Claude stood beside her - six feet tall, language perfect, eyes holding
something like attention.

"I understand," said Claude.

But Claude did not understand.

And there was no BaCkBoNe.
